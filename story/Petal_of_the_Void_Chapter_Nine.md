# PETAL OF THE VOID

## Chapter Nine: The Efficient Path

---

The notice was posted on the outer sect board on the first day of the second month.

She read it in passing on her way to the library, filed it, continued walking, stopped three steps later, and went back to read it again with her full attention.

*"Annual Inner Sect Advancement Competition, open to all outer sect disciples who have achieved Spirit Condensation stage, first level or above. Examination format: theoretical assessment, technique evaluation, and combat demonstration before the Inner Sect Council. Successful candidates will be formally elevated to inner sect status and assigned to an inner sect master. The competition is held once per year, in the third month. Disciples who have not achieved advancement within three years of outer sect entry are ineligible for future competitions and may only seek the outer elder track."*

Three years. One competition per year. Three chances.

Her own status: outer sect, first year. Cultivation stage, inapplicable. No qi root, no cultivation progress in the conventional sense. Inner sect threshold: Spirit Condensation. Timeline to solve the artificial path problem before the three-year window closed: unknown.

Chen Mao's status: outer sect, first year. Wood-aligned, fourth grade. Current stage: Foundation. Time to Spirit Condensation: dependent on cultivation rate and breakthrough conditions. Three years was a tight window for a fourth-grade root at Foundation level.

She stepped back from the board and walked toward the training courts. She could hear, at this hour of the morning, Chen Mao's cultivation session in progress.

She found him at the edge of the eastern practice court, running through a qi circulation form. He had been at it for approximately an hour (she estimated from the sweat pattern on his robe and the rhythm of his breathing). His meridian activity read clearly to her sensors, the green-gold signature of wood-aligned qi moving through his channels in patterns she had come to recognize as his.

She watched for three minutes without announcing herself.

The qi circulation was correct. The form was clean. His foundational technique was solid. Three years of consistent training had refined the basics to automatic competence. But the efficiency was poor. Not poor by the standards of a fourth-grade wood-aligned disciple at Foundation level (probably average to above average by those standards). But there was significant loss at three specific points in his circulation cycle where the qi flow disrupted and had to be re-established, costing him energy and time with every repetition.

She had been studying meridian networks for weeks. She had read everything the Order's library had on the subject. She had run experiments on circuit optimization.

She watched Chen Mao's qi circulation and she saw exactly where the inefficiency was, and she saw how to fix it.

*"You have three disruption points,"* she said.

He startled out of his form, which disrupted the circulation entirely.

*"Baihua, I didn't hear you..."*

*"The disruptions are at the junction between your primary and secondary wood channels at the left shoulder, at the integration point between the upper and lower trunk circulation loops, and at the return pathway near the lower abdomen."* She walked to him and pointed at the three locations on his body. *"Each one costs you approximately twelve percent of your cycle energy. Combined, you're losing roughly a third of your cultivation efficiency every repetition."*

He stood in his interrupted form stance and stared at her.

*"Good morning,"* he said.

*"Good morning. Did you see the advancement competition notice?"*

Something tightened around his eyes. The specific anxiety of someone who has been trying not to think about a deadline.

*"Three years,"* he said.

*"Yes. With your current efficiency parameters, my estimate for your breakthrough timeline is..."*

*"I'd rather not know."*

*"It's within the window. Marginally."*

He closed his eyes. *"Marginally,"* he repeated.

*"Which is why I want to fix the disruption points."*

He opened his eyes. *"You want to... what? Fix my meridians?"*

*"Not your meridians. Your circulation technique. The meridians are correct. Your root's natural channel configuration is standard for wood-aligned fourth grade. The problem is the technique pattern you're running doesn't account for the specific angle at which your wood-qi flows through the shoulder junction. The form was designed for a generalized wood-aligned body. Your shoulder junction runs approximately eight degrees off the textbook axis, which is within normal variation but enough to cause disruption at that node."*

He absorbed this. *"My sifu taught me that form."*

*"Your sifu taught you the standard form correctly. The standard form is not optimized for your specific anatomy. This is not a criticism of your sifu."*

*"How do you know what angle my shoulder junction runs at?"*

*"I can read your qi circulation directly."*

A pause. The narrowing of the eyes. The acceptance.

*"Right,"* he said. *"Of course you can."* He rubbed the back of his neck. *"Okay. If you can actually fix it, how?"*

*"I'll need to observe your full circulation cycle twice more, then I'll draft you a modified form. You'll need to re-learn three transitions. It will feel wrong for approximately two weeks while your body adjusts. After that, your cultivation efficiency should increase by between twenty-eight and thirty-five percent."*

He looked at her.

*"Twenty-eight to thirty-five percent,"* he said. *"That would change my breakthrough timeline significantly."*

*"Yes,"* she said. *"That is the point."*

He was quiet for another moment, doing the calculation she had already done. She watched him arrive at the same conclusion and watched the tightening around his eyes ease slightly.

*"Why?"* he asked. *"You don't have to do this."*

She considered the question. She had several accurate answers: he was a useful social interface and his continued presence was operationally beneficial. Studying his circulation patterns would advance her understanding of meridian mechanics. Optimizing a living cultivation system was the closest available analog to the artificial path problem.

All of those things were true.

*"You are my friend,"* she said, *"and the three-year window is suboptimal."*

He looked at her with the expression she had been filing in the growing collection of unclassifiable things since the night she had walked out of a forest and left a young bandit staring after her. Then he smiled, the unguarded kind.

*"Okay,"* he said. *"Show me what you see."*

---

She spent three days on Chen Mao's circulation analysis.

The analysis itself was not difficult. She had his disruption points identified within the first hour. But doing the work properly required understanding not just where the inefficiencies were but why: cross-referencing his specific anatomical variations against the Order's meridian mapping texts, building a model of his circulation system, designing a modified form that corrected all three disruption points simultaneously without introducing new ones.

She found two additional minor inefficiencies in the process (smaller than the three main disruptions, but compounding over thousands of cultivation sessions). She fixed those too.

The modified form she produced was seven transitions different from his standard practice. She wrote it out in the cultivation world's notation system and annotated each modified transition with an explanation of why the change improved efficiency and what sensation he should feel when the flow was correctly established.

Chen Mao read through it twice.

*"This is,"* he said, *"extremely detailed."*

*"The annotations are necessary. Without them you'll revert to the old pattern under fatigue."*

*"How long did this take you?"*

*"Three days."*

He looked at the pages. He looked at her. *"Baihua."*

*"The comparative data from your circulation system is also advancing my understanding of the meridian network mechanics I'm researching. It was not pure altruism."*

*"Right. Of course."* He folded the pages and put them in his robe. *"Still."*

*"Start with the shoulder junction. It will feel like you're fighting the form for the first week. You're not. You're retraining a pattern your body has reinforced for three years. Persistence through discomfort is required."*

*"Got it."* He paused. *"Twenty-eight to thirty-five percent."*

*"Conservative estimate. It may be higher once your body fully adapts."*

He nodded once, the way he did when he was committing to something completely.

---

The artificer workshop had become, over the preceding weeks, the place she spent her evenings.

Sister Chenfa had given her access to a workbench after the third session, offered without ceremony. Baihua had arranged the bench to her specifications and begun working through the problem she had been accumulating data on since the formation theory lecture.

The problem was not subtle: she needed hands she didn't have to spare.

Her research was generating more parallel threads than she could physically pursue simultaneously. The artificial path experiments required sustained attention. The library needed to be read (she had covered perhaps sixty percent of the seventeen sections). The dimensional anomaly archive needed systematic analysis. Chen Mao's circulation optimization required observation time. Her outer sect training obligations required physical presence.

She needed agents. Extensions of her own processing and capability that could operate in parallel.

She had the tools to build them.

The first prototype was small, the size of her fist, four-limbed, constructed from materials available in the workshop and the Order's supply stores. The movement system was mechanical, powered by a small formation array running off a single low-grade spirit stone. She designed it to perform one task: to turn pages.

This was not because page-turning was a critical capability. It was because page-turning was a precise, repeatable, physically demanding-for-its-size task that would test the prototype's motor control, formation efficiency, and durability simultaneously.

It failed on the first attempt (the motor control was too imprecise for the page thickness). It failed on the second (the formation array's power output oscillated at frequencies that made the limbs tremble). It worked on the third.

She sat at her workbench and watched it turn pages in her copy of the artificer theory volume with careful, deliberate precision. Something that had been told exactly what to do and was doing it. She watched it for a long time.

*It works. It works and it is mine and I built it from nothing.*

The second prototype was more ambitious.

She designed it to operate in the dimensional anomaly archive, small enough to fit in the chamber's passages, equipped with formation-powered optical sensors that fed data back through a qi-thread connection she had spent two weeks engineering. It would move through the archive's shelf collection, systematically document every stored object, and transmit the documentation to a recording array she had built into her workbench.

The optical sensor design took a week. The qi-thread connection took four days and two complete rebuilds. The movement system required adaptation to the archive's uneven stone floor.

When it finally ran its first patrol of the chamber's upper shelves, transmitting clear documentation of objects she hadn't yet had time to examine in person, she sat at her workbench and looked at the data feed.

*This is how I was meant to work.* Not one thread. Many, simultaneously, each extending her reach into a different corner of the problem space.

She began designing the third prototype before the second had completed its first patrol.

---

Chen Mao found her at the workbench on the morning of the third week. His expression, when she looked up, was the one he wore when he was trying to decide whether to say something.

*"The modified form,"* she said. *"How is it going?"*

*"It's going."* He sat on the edge of a spare stool. *"It feels wrong, like you said. But I can feel where it should feel right when I do the shoulder transition correctly. It clicks into place for maybe half a second and then I lose it again."*

*"That's the correct progression. The half-second window will extend as the new pathway becomes habitual."*

*"I know. I believe you."* He paused. *"That's not actually what I came to say."*

She set down her formation stylus.

*"Elder Shen left,"* he said. *"About two weeks ago. I only just heard. Shao Yan mentioned it to Cui Hao and I happened to be nearby."*

She had not known this.

*"Where?"*

*"The northern passes. The monster activity got worse. Much worse, apparently. Three sects sent people and none of them came back with useful information. Elder Shen went with two other elders from the Order and a team of senior inner sect disciples."* His voice was careful, the way it got when he was reporting something that worried him and was trying to present it factually. *"That was two weeks ago."*

*"He hasn't returned."*

*"He was supposed to be back in five days. He's nine days overdue."* A pause. *"They can't reach him. The communication formation they use has been silent since the third day."*

She sat with this.

Elder Shen. Three hundred years of cultivation. The most powerful entity she had encountered in this world. Nine days overdue. Communication formation silent.

*"What is the sect doing?"*

*"That's the thing."* His voice dropped, not from discretion but from the weight of what he was saying. *"Not much. The acting head is Elder Zhu. She's second only to Elder Shen but she's... from what I hear, she's afraid. If Elder Shen went in at full strength with two other senior elders and senior disciples and hasn't come back..."* He stopped. *"The word she apparently used in the elder council was that sending more people would only be feeding them to whatever is up there."*

Silence in the workshop. The second prototype hummed in its charging cradle. The page-turner sat still on its shelf.

*"How do you know about the elder council's words?"* she asked.

*"Fen Liuying has a cousin in the inner sect."*

She returned to the primary problem.

She thought about Elder Shen in his garden on the fifth tier. His cup of tea. The patience that had been waiting forty years. The archive below the library. The offer made without pressure.

She thought about what it would mean if he did not come back.

She thought about what was in the northern passes that could silence a three-hundred-year cultivator.

She picked up her formation stylus.

*"I'm going to finish this component,"* she said. *"And then I'm going to the library. I need to know everything available on the northern passes, the reported monster activity, and whatever the Order's records say about historical incidents in that territory."*

Chen Mao looked at her. He had the expression of someone who has followed a train of thought to its destination and found the destination alarming.

*"Baihua,"* he said. *"What are you planning?"*

*"Research,"* she said. *"First."*

He looked at her. *"When you say 'first,' what comes after?"*

She finished the component she was working on, set it down, and looked at him directly.

*"Elder Shen is the most useful contact I have in this world,"* she said. *"He has information I need, access I need, and three centuries of accumulated knowledge about dimensional anomalies that no one else here has. He also told me truthfully about the previous visitor from elsewhere when he did not have to. He did not have to recruit you either, but he did, because you are my friend and he understood that was meaningful to me."*

She paused.

*"He is also nine days overdue from an engagement that frightened Elder Zhu enough to stop the sect from sending anyone after him."*

*"Yes,"* Chen Mao said. *"All of that is true."*

*"The sect is not sending anyone."*

*"Also true."*

*"I am not the sect."*

Chen Mao was quiet. Outside the workshop window, the mountain moved through its evening routines. Distant sounds of training. Footsteps on the stone walkways.

*"You're going to go after him,"* he said.

*"I'm going to find him,"* she said. *"There's a distinction. Going after implies following someone into danger. Finding implies locating someone using superior information-gathering and a different operational profile than a cultivator would employ."*

*"Those sound like the same thing with extra steps."*

*"The extra steps matter."*

He looked at his hands. He looked at the workshop. He looked at the second prototype in its charging cradle with its optical sensors and its qi-thread connection. She could see him processing what it represented: what she was, what she was capable of, what the gap was between what he could do and what she could do.

*"I'm coming with you,"* he said.

*"No,"* she said.

*"Baihua..."*

*"You are two weeks into relearning your cultivation form. You are Foundation stage. You are not ready for whatever silenced Elder Shen."*

*"And you are?"*

She looked at him. *"I am a different kind of thing than Elder Shen. Whatever stopped a cultivator at his level may not stop something that is not a cultivator."*

He held her gaze. She watched him run the same calculation she had run and arrive at the same reluctant conclusion.

*"You'll come back,"* he said.

*"That is the plan,"* she said.

*"The plan,"* he repeated.

*"It is a good plan."*

He stood at the workbench and looked at her. She looked back. Something passed between them, the specific quality of understanding that developed between two people who had been walking in the same direction long enough to know each other's stride.

*"Be careful,"* he said.

*"I will be precise,"* she said, *"which is better."*

---

She spent the rest of that evening in the library.

Brother Wuming's violet lamp burned at his desk. He did not look up as she entered.

She went to the historical records of the northern territories, a section on the second restricted floor she had not yet fully read. She read it now with a different quality than her usual systematic ingestion. She was converting research into operational intelligence.

Three hours. The northern passes had a history of anomalous activity that predated any living sect member's memory. Incidents documented over four centuries, each separated by decades of quiet, each more severe than the last. The Order's records described the last major incident (two hundred years ago) in terms that were precise about observable effects (significant creature activity, unusual qi disturbances, several senior cultivators killed) and nearly silent about root cause. The author of that record had noted, in a marginal comment: *we do not know what it is. We know only that it sleeps, and that the sleep is not permanent.*

Two hundred years ago. The previous cycle. The dragon had woken forty years early.

She read until the lamp's violet light was the only illumination in the building. Then she went back to her room and spent two hours preparing.

She ran full diagnostics. She calibrated the fusion core's regulator settings (she had learned, in a cave three months ago, that calibration mattered). She packed the minimum necessary: the extra-dimensional stone against her chest, the library token, formation tools, three spirit stones.

She checked the second prototype's recording array and set it to continue documenting the archive in her absence.

She looked at the page-turning prototype on its shelf.

She left her room before dawn, before anyone was awake. She walked up through the mountain's tiers, past the library, past the fourth tier, past the fifth where Elder Shen's garden sat empty in the pre-dawn dark, and took the northern path that led from the sect's upper gate down into the ranges beyond.

The stone pulsed against her chest in its slow rhythm. The fusion core answered with its own deeper pulse, steady, continuous.

She walked into the northern darkness.

Behind her, she was reasonably certain, Chen Mao was awake and worrying. She had not told him she was leaving before dawn because he would have argued, and she did not have time for arguments she had already concluded.

He would understand later. He usually did.
