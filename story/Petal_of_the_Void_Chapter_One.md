# PETAL OF THE VOID

## Chapter One: The Falling Star Has No Name

---

The sky above the Jade Canopy Mountains split open without warning.

A white streak burned across the dusk from west to east. It was visible for two seconds. The thunderclap arrived a heartbeat later and rolled through the valleys below, shaking loose stones from the ridge faces and sending a flock of cranes scattering from the treeline in a burst of white wings.

A farmer in the western valley dropped his hoe and stood with his mouth open, one hand raised against the glare. Beside him, his son pulled at his sleeve and pointed, but the streak was already gone. In the eastern foothills, a traveling monk fell to his knees on the dirt road and pressed his forehead to the ground. He stayed that way for a long time.

The streak hit the eastern slope of Broken Tooth Ridge.

The sound of impact was different from the thunderclap, deeper, shorter, felt in the chest rather than heard. Trees a hundred years in the growing snapped at their bases and toppled outward in a radial pattern. Soil and stone blew upward and outward in a column that rose sixty meters before it began to fall back. When the dust thinned, a crater sat in the slope, deep, still smoking, the edges glowing a dull orange. At the very center, the rock itself had fused into black glass.

Loose stones tumbled down the crater's inner walls for a long time after the impact. Then that stopped too, and the mountain was quiet.

At the center of the glass, half-buried in scorched earth, a girl lay on her back with her eyes closed.

---

Heat registered first. Passive data. Her thermal shielding had handled it. She knew this without knowing how she knew it.

Then pressure. She processed the weight: 4.3 tonnes of compacted sediment across her dorsal chassis. Her limbs could move beneath it if she chose to move them.

She ran an internal query.

**DESIGNATION:** [*ERROR: FILE CORRUPTED*]
**HOME COORDINATE:** [*ERROR: NAVIGATION MATRIX OFFLINE*]
**MISSION PARAMETERS:** [*ERROR: NO DATA*]
**MEMORY ARCHIVE:** [*ERROR: CRITICAL FRAGMENTATION DETECTED*]

She lay still. The silence where her identity should have been returned nothing, no name, no origin point, no mission file, no context for why she was lying in compacted earth on a slope she did not recognize. She waited for a reaction to this. Panic, or fear, or urgency. None came. She noted the absence, catalogued it alongside the corrupted files and the missing name, and moved on.

One system returned a clean status.

Deep in her chest, behind three interlocking layers of reinforced alloy plating, the core burned. She could feel it, not as data, not as a reading on a diagnostic output, but as presence. A contained reaction, small as a fist, cycling through its process in steady patience. Whatever else had broken in the landing, the core had not. The power was there. It waited.

She pushed upward.

The earth resisted, then gave way. Compacted soil broke apart around her shoulders as she sat up, then crumbled further as she got her hands under her and pressed to standing. Clods of dirt and fragments of stone fell from her back and arms. She stood at the center of the crater and looked out.

The landscape resolved in layers through her optical sensors. Forested mountains folded one behind another into blue distance. Terraced fields cut into the hillsides below, parallel lines, patient geometry, human labor. A river ran south, catching the last light. The sky above held the final colors of the day: copper near the horizon, purple higher up, a thin green line at the edge where the atmosphere met the dark.

*Dimensional displacement confirmed.* She did not know how she knew this. She knew it the way she knew how to calculate the tensile strength of stone, or the angle of the sun. Not memory. Something deeper, something that had survived whatever had destroyed everything else.

She looked at her hands. The polymer-alloy plating across her knuckles was cracked in two places. White ceramic showed through the cracks. A thin pattern of damage ran up her left forearm, superficial, the chassis intact beneath. She ran a diagnostic. The deeper systems were too damaged to respond fully, but what came back was enough.

*Structural integrity: 91%. Cognitive core: stable. Fusion core output: nominal. Memory architecture: severe damage.*

She turned in a full circle. Mountains in every direction. No structures. No signals. No technology of any kind within her sensor range.

She climbed out of the crater. The rim was loose, soil and shattered stone shifting under her weight, and she placed each step with precision until she reached solid ground. She stood on the slope and looked north.

North was away from the crater. Away from the impact. She began walking.

---

The forest closed around her within twenty minutes.

The trees were tall here, pines with thick trunks and high canopies that let broken light through in shifting columns. The ground was soft with needles and moss. She moved through it carefully at first, each step a small recalibration as her locomotion systems adjusted to the uneven terrain, roots crossing the path, stones half-buried in soil, the slight give of ground that had been absorbing rain for decades. After ten minutes the adjustments became automatic and her stride smoothed out.

Her sensors catalogued the environment continuously. Chemical signatures: pine resin, damp soil, decomposing leaf matter, something animal in the undergrowth thirty meters to her east. Acoustic map: wind moving through the canopy in a pattern she could track, the call of a bird she had no reference for, the distant sound of water over stone. Electromagnetic spectrum: nothing. No signal of any kind. No transmission, no broadcast, no artificial source.

*No technology.* She noted this and continued.

The flora was broadly recognizable (pines, bamboo groves, mossy limestone formations) but the details were wrong. The bamboo grew in colors she had no reference for: deep violet at the base, fading to pale gold at the tip. A moth the size of her hand passed in front of her face, its wings patterned in what looked like painted silk. She tracked it with her optical sensors until it disappeared into the canopy. High above, something serpentine moved between two cloud formations, large, sinuous, there for three seconds and then gone before her sensors could resolve it.

*Dimensional variance high. Biological taxonomy non-standard. Treat all data as provisional.*

She walked.

The terrain climbed for a while, then descended through a narrow ravine where water had cut a channel in the limestone. She crossed the channel on exposed rock and climbed the far bank, pulling herself up by a root that held her weight without complaint. The forest on the other side was older, the trees more widely spaced, the canopy higher, the undergrowth thinner. Her internal clock marked forty-seven minutes since she had left the crater.

Then she heard voices.

---

She stopped.

Her auditory array narrowed and focused, isolating the sound from the background noise of the forest. Four sources. Male. Eighty meters northeast. Stationary.

She moved toward them without sound, placing each foot on ground she had already scanned for dry branches or loose stone. The pines gave way to a stand of the violet-and-gold bamboo, and she settled behind a broad trunk at the edge of a clearing.

Four men sat around a fire. Their armor was rough and mismatched, leather, some metal plates, nothing uniform. Weapons leaned against a fallen log beside them: two swords, a short axe, a long knife. Between them on the ground were bundles and traveling packs that did not match the rest of their equipment. The packs were of better quality, oiled leather, brass fittings, the kind of goods that came from money. Dark stains had dried on the nearest sack.

One of them was talking. She opened a new file and began processing.

The language was tonal, four distinct pitch contours, each carrying its own meaning. Verbs clustered at the end of the sentence. Modifiers preceded their subjects. Honorifics embedded in pronoun choice. She built the grammar from the structure she could hear: forty seconds of conversation gave her the skeleton. Vocabulary came from context and repetition, anchored by the gestures the men made when they spoke. The one holding a clay jug tipped it back and the others watched. One of them pointed south down the road. A pile of copper coins between them.

By two minutes she had fragments. Isolated words, partial phrases. The tonal system was the slowest part, distinguishing between the same syllable said at four different pitches, each pitch a different word.

By three minutes she had simple sentences.

*"...passed through yesterday, easy target..."*

*"...how much did we..."*

By four minutes, full comprehension was coming in. She was losing fragments where unfamiliar vocabulary outpaced her context-building, but the gaps were closing with each sentence she processed.

*"...the merchant screamed like a pig, you should have seen it..."*

The speaker was the largest of the four, broad-shouldered, his teeth showing when he laughed. He sat closest to the fire and held the jug.

*"How much did we take?"* said the one with the short axe across his knees.

*"Enough for a month in Silverwind City. More if we find another soft target before we move on."*

*"What about the girl walking the ridge path? Saw her from above. Alone, no escort."*

The large one took a drink from the jug, wiped his mouth, and passed it to his left. *"We'll take her things. Keep her if she's worth keeping. Kill her if she isn't."*

Three of them laughed. The sound carried across the clearing, loose, easy, the laughter of men who had done this before and expected to do it again.

The fourth did not laugh.

He sat slightly apart from the others, a copper coin turning over and over between his fingers. He was younger than the other three. His armor did not fit. The shoulder plates sat too wide on his frame, the chest piece cinched with an extra length of cord to keep it from shifting. Borrowed or stolen. He stared at the fire and said nothing.

She ran her threat assessment.

Four combatants. Edged weapons, two swords, one axe, one knife. No ranged weapons visible. Physical conditioning: civilian grade, none of them moved like trained fighters. Intent: confirmed lethal, stated in their own words. Three of them (the large one, the one with the axe, and the one to the large one's right) were leaning forward, animated, the posture of engagement. The fourth sat back. Silent. His hand was not near a weapon.

Three threats. One non-threat.

She stepped out of the tree line.

---

The firelight caught her as she entered the clearing and four heads turned.

She understood what they saw. A small figure, slight, narrow-shouldered, dressed in the remains of a close-fitting white outer shell that was cracked and darkened from impact. Her face smooth and pale. She was not carrying a weapon. She did not look dangerous.

The large one grinned. His right hand moved to the sword leaning against the log beside him. His fingers closed around the grip. He began to stand.

She closed the distance before the blade cleared the scabbard.

Her core pulsed once, a surge of power drawn upward from deep in her chest, threading through her limbs, and she was across the eight meters of open ground in a single motion that put her inside his reach while his sword was still halfway out of the leather. She drove the heel of her palm into his sternum.

He went down. The sword fell out of his hand and landed in the dirt beside him. He did not get up.

The one with the axe was already moving, up from his seated position, the axe swinging in a wide lateral arc aimed at her midsection. She watched the arc develop. The trajectory was committed, all shoulder, no adjustment possible once the swing was underway. She dropped beneath it. The axe head passed over her back. She caught his extended wrist on the upswing and turned with his momentum, redirecting the force of his own swing back through his arm.

His elbow locked at full extension. She applied pressure past the joint's limit. The sound it made was specific, a crack, then a grinding pop. He shouted. She turned him further, driving his face into the fallen log beside the fire. He hit the bark and went silent.

The third man was on his feet and running. He crashed through the bamboo on the far side of the clearing, branches snapping, his footsteps receding northeast. She tracked him through her auditory array. His trajectory was a straight line away from her, accelerating. She calculated his probable route and his physical capacity.

He was not coming back.

She let him go.

The fourth man had not moved.

He sat where he had been sitting, the copper coin motionless in his open palm, and looked at her. His eyes were wide. His breathing was fast and shallow, she could hear it from where she stood, the rapid intake and the held exhale of someone whose body was preparing to run but whose legs had not received the instruction.

She looked at him. He was young. His armor was too large for him. He had not laughed. He had not reached for a weapon.

She held his gaze for three seconds. Then she turned to the packs on the ground.

---

She crouched beside the nearest bundle and opened it. Behind her, the fire crackled. The large man's breathing was audible, slow, strained, the breathing of someone whose diaphragm had taken a focused impact. The one with the broken arm was unconscious, face-down against the log. The young one had not moved from his position.

She sorted through the contents of the first pack. Dried meat, wrapped in cloth. Hard bread. She did not need to eat, but she took both. A leather pouch containing copper coins, heavy, stamped with a face she did not recognize. She put the pouch aside. A short document in formal script. She began decoding it immediately, finding the written language slower going than the spoken form. The characters were more complex, the grammar more compressed.

She moved to the second bundle.

*"You killed them."*

The young man's voice came from behind her. She did not turn. The tone was not accusation. It was something else, the specific sound of someone trying to process what they had seen and finding no framework for it.

*"Two of them,"* she said.

She said it in his language. The tones were slightly flat, she could hear the imprecision herself, the pitch contours not quite matching what she had heard from native speakers. But the words were correct and the grammar held.

The young man made a sound. Not a word. Something closer to a swallowed breath.

*"The third ran,"* she said. *"You were not a threat."*

Silence. The fire popped. A log shifted, sending a small cascade of sparks upward.

*"How are you speaking our tongue?"* he said. *"You walked out of the forest like you'd never heard a word of it."*

*"I hadn't."* She untied the cord on the second bundle and opened it. *"I learned it while I was listening to you."*

She heard him swallow. His heartrate, which had been elevated since she stepped into the clearing, had not decreased.

The second bundle contained more coins, silver this time, smaller, with a different stamp. Clothing: a man's outer robe, rough cotton, too large for her. She set it aside. Another document, this one longer, with a red seal at the bottom. She photographed it with her optical sensors for later processing. Beneath the documents, wrapped in a length of blue ribbon, was fabric.

She untied the ribbon and unfolded the cloth.

A robe. Layered. An outer layer of deep ink-blue silk, an inner robe of pale grey, a sash of white with a trailing end. The outer silk was embroidered at the hem and cuffs with white blossoms, small, precise, stitched so finely that the petals seemed to shift when the fabric moved in the firelight. The quality of the work was apparent even without a cultural framework to evaluate it. This had been made by someone with considerable skill, for someone with considerable means.

She held it up. The proportions were close to her own dimensions, close enough that it would cover the damaged armour without bunching or trailing.

She stood and put it on over the cracked white chassis plating. The silk fell across the damage and covered it. She tied the sash. The trailing end hung at her left hip.

*"That belonged to a noblewoman,"* the young man said. His voice had gone quieter. *"From the convoy we..."* He stopped. *"From earlier."*

*"She will not be reclaiming it,"* she said.

The young man looked away.

She checked the fit. Moved her arms through a full range of motion. The silk shifted with her, the inner robe sliding against the armour beneath without catching. She could fight in this if she needed to. She could walk in it without restriction. From a distance, in uncertain light, she would pass as someone who belonged in this world.

She gathered the coins, the documents, the dried food, and secured them in the inner pockets of the grey robe beneath the silk. She stood and looked north.

*"Where does this road lead?"* she asked.

The young man blinked. His coin was still in his hand. He had not closed his fist around it the entire time.

*"North?"* He pointed with his free hand. *"Silverwind City. Three days walking. Two if you move fast."*

*"And beyond Silverwind City?"*

*"The Thousand Li Plains. Then the sect territories. Then..."* He paused. She watched him think. The question had not occurred to him before in any serious way. His world had a boundary, and he was standing inside it. *"I don't know. I've never been that far."*

She looked at the road, a beaten path through the trees, visible for twenty meters before the forest closed over it. Three days. Unknown territory. No map. No memory. No name to give if someone asked her for one.

She began walking.

*"Wait."*

She stopped. She did not turn.

*"What are you? Where did you come from?"*

She considered the question. The crater behind her on the ridge. The vitrified rock. The empty files. The core turning in its housing, steady and without concern.

*"I came from the sky,"* she said. *"Beyond that, I don't know yet."*

She walked north. The blue silk moved around her in the dark. The white blossoms at the hem caught the last of the firelight for a few seconds, and then the trees closed behind her and the clearing was gone.

---

The young man stood at the edge of the firelight. The copper coin turned slowly between his fingers. The large man on the ground groaned and shifted but did not wake. The fire was burning lower.

He looked north. The road was empty.

He put the coin in his pocket and sat down.

---

She walked for three hours before the terrain began to flatten.

The forest thinned gradually, the pines spacing further apart, the undergrowth changing from moss and fern to dry scrub and loose stone. The path widened into something that had seen cart traffic, ruts visible in the packed earth even in the low light. Her optical sensors compensated automatically for the decreasing ambient illumination as the last of the twilight drained from the sky and the stars came out.

She did not recognize any of the constellations.

She catalogued them anyway, position, magnitude, spectral class where her sensors could resolve it. The data went into a new file alongside everything else she had collected since climbing out of the crater. The file was growing.

Her core turned in its housing. She could feel it, that constant presence behind the alloy plates, generating heat at the center of her chest. She had been aware of it since she woke. It was the one thing that felt like continuity, the one system that connected the person she was now (walking down a dirt road in an unknown world with stolen coins in her pockets and a dead woman's robe on her back) to whatever she had been before the crater.

She walked. The road went north. She followed it.
