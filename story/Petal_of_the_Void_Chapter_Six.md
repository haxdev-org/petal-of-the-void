# PETAL OF THE VOID

## Chapter Six: The Weight of Watching Eyes

---

The combat grounds were larger than the examination plaza.

A dozen marked circles of packed earth spread across the broad field outside the city's northern wall, each one ringed with viewing space for observers. The whole arrangement was overlooked by a long tiered gallery where the sect representatives sat in a row, their attention distributed across every circle at once. She could read the direction of their focus through their meridian activity (the faint thermal shifts that accompanied active perception) and confirmed that at least three of them were monitoring multiple circles simultaneously.

She stood at the edge of the field with Chen Mao and watched the morning's first round begin.

The format was straightforward: names drawn by lot, matched pairs assigned to circles, combat until one party yielded or could not continue. No weapons in the preliminary rounds. Hands, feet, qi. Clean elimination. The sect representatives watched everything and selected from the pool of winners as rounds progressed.

*Multiple matches. Multiple opportunities to calibrate.*

She reviewed her calibration settings one final time. She had spent two days working on this. The band of acceptable performance was narrow: she had to win consistently, across an unknown number of rounds against opponents of increasing capability, while appearing to be exactly what she had presented herself as. A fifth-grade metal-aligned Foundation cultivator. Gifted, controlled, exceptional, but human.

Her name was called.

---

Her first opponent was a fire-aligned boy of perhaps seventeen with quick feet and the aggressive forward pressure of someone who had trained against opponents slower than him. He was good. His opening rush (a burst of speed trailing heat shimmer, aimed at closing distance before a defensive cultivator could set) would have been effective against an ordinary Foundation-level candidate.

She let him get most of the way there.

She sidestepped at the last moment that a human cultivator of her presented level could plausibly manage, used the momentum of his pass to redirect him off-balance, and followed with a controlled palm strike to his shoulder. He spun and went to one knee. She stepped back into a defensive stance and waited.

He scrambled upright and came again, faster, fire qi flickering at his fingertips. She absorbed his strike on her forearm (surface ceramic temperature elevated, within tolerance), turned with it, and caught him in a joint lock that she held until he tapped the ground.

Twelve seconds total. She had stretched it to twelve deliberately, inserting two apparent near-misses that were not near-misses.

In the gallery, she registered the sound of notes being made.

---

Her second opponent was older, mid-twenties, water-aligned, with the fluid evasive style that the cultivation texts associated with advanced water technique. Water-aligned fighters did not commit. They moved around strikes rather than blocking them, redirected rather than absorbed. A fight against a skilled water cultivator could go on for a long time.

She decided to let it.

She gave him four exchanges that he won or drew on his own merits. Genuine exchanges, where she operated within her stated parameters and his technique was good enough to handle her. He was skilled. She noted this.

On the fifth exchange she began to close the gap. She increased her output incrementally across three more engagements until she had created an opening her parameters said a fifth-grade metal cultivator could plausibly exploit. She took it: a shoulder throw into a ground control position that she held until the official called the match.

Forty-seven seconds. The gallery was paying closer attention now.

---

Her third opponent was at Spirit Condensation stage, the first she had faced in a controlled context. Spirit Condensation fighters operated differently from Foundation-level ones: qi projection, expanded sensory range, speed that exceeded Foundation ceiling by a meaningful margin. She adjusted her performance parameters upward by the exact amount required to win without revealing that the adjustment was trivial.

The fight lasted thirty-one seconds. The Spirit Condensation candidate walked off the circle looking back at her twice.

Her fourth opponent was a dual-aligned candidate, fire and earth, with strong fundamentals and a creative combination style. She let him demonstrate his best work for thirty seconds of genuine exchange, then closed the fight with a sequence that her parameters rated as plausible for an exceptional Foundation cultivator having a very good day.

By the afternoon she had won four matches. The field had thinned. The weaker candidates were gone. The survivors were distributed across fewer circles, watched with proportionally more focus.

Chen Mao found her between rounds.

*"Three wins,"* he said. He was slightly winded, his face bright. *"I got three wins. The third one was against a metal-aligned guy who was stronger than me, but I used the environmental technique, the one where you grow vines from the ground edge, and he wasn't expecting it at all."*

*"Good,"* she said.

*"How many have you won?"*

*"Four."*

He stared at her. *"You beat a Spirit Condensation cultivator."*

*"Yes."*

*"You're Foundation level."*

*"Technically."*

The narrowing of the eyes. The decision to accept it. She appreciated that reflex in him.

*"The Iron Peak representative,"* he said, lowering his voice, *"has been watching your circle since your second match. I can see him from where I'm waiting. He hasn't looked away."*

*"I know,"* she said. *"There are currently four sect representatives watching this circle. Not just Iron Peak."*

Chen Mao looked at the gallery. Looked back at her. *"How do you know that?"*

*"I can tell."*

He opened his mouth. Closed it. *"Right,"* he said. *"Of course you can."*

---

Her fifth match was against the strongest opponent she had faced in the trials.

Luo Fengying. Sword-aligned, Spirit Condensation stage, independent. Several years older than Baihua presented. She wore no sect colors. She handled her sword with the easy familiarity of a longtime companion. She had a quality of stillness at her center that Baihua had learned to associate with someone who had been training since childhood and had never once wasted a session.

They stood across from each other in the circle. Luo Fengying looked at her with eyes that were doing the same thing Baihua's were doing: assessing, cataloguing, building a picture.

*"Foundation level,"* Luo Fengying said. Not dismissive. Thoughtful.

*"Yes,"* Baihua said.

*"Your previous matches were interesting to watch."*

*"Thank you."*

*"That wasn't entirely a compliment."* Luo Fengying's hand rested near her sword hilt, loose, the opposite of tension. *"You're not fighting like Foundation level."*

*"I fight like myself,"* Baihua said.

The official signaled the start.

Luo Fengying drew.

---

The sword came across fast, a diagonal cut with qi projection extending the blade's effective reach by half a meter. Baihua moved inside the projection's leading edge and blocked the physical blade on her left forearm. The ceramic plating stressed with the contact. The qi projection washed over her shoulder and dissipated.

Luo Fengying recovered and came again, a different angle, a thrust that transitioned into a lateral sweep when Baihua shifted her weight. The sweep missed by the margin Baihua intended.

She let this match breathe. Luo Fengying was the best human fighter she had encountered in this world and the data was worth collecting in full. She fought it with the closest thing to genuine engagement she had experienced since the serpent in the cave.

Luo Fengying adapted between exchanges. When the first combination didn't work, she changed her approach. When the second combination didn't work, she changed it again. She read patterns, adjusted timing, tested different angles. She fought with intelligence as much as skill.

The match ran longer than any of Baihua's previous bouts. She blocked strikes on her forearms, feeling the ceramic plating stress with each contact. She moved through Luo Fengying's combinations with a precision that her opponents kept reading as calm (because it was calm, perfect, absolute, the calm of something that was not physiologically capable of adrenaline or fear) while maintaining continuous micro-adjustment to stay within her performance parameters.

The seventh exchange. Luo Fengying overcommitted on a thrust by three centimeters, enough to extend her recovery window by a fraction of a second. Baihua stepped inside the sword's reach, caught the blade arm at the wrist, and positioned her other hand at Luo Fengying's throat with enough evident force to make the outcome clear.

A breath.

*"Yield,"* Luo Fengying said. Then, quieter, for the two of them only: *"What are you?"*

Baihua released her and stepped back.

*"A traveler,"* she said. *"From the west."*

Luo Fengying looked at her for a long moment. She resheathed her sword and walked off the circle with her back straight and her face composed.

---

She won her sixth and final match against a solid, earnest earth-aligned cultivator who had gotten this far on determination rather than talent. She was gentle about it. He had earned his place in the final rounds. She used a simple sweep and hold that ended the match in eight seconds without leaving visible marks.

When the official marked her through, she stood in the circle and looked up at the gallery.

Seven sect representatives. All of them looking back at her.

She walked off the circle.

---

The first approach came from Iron Peak, a broad-shouldered senior disciple in grey-and-black robes who delivered the sect's interest in the specific tone of someone who considered social niceties a regrettable necessity. Iron Peak had noted her combat record. Iron Peak valued combat above scholarship. Iron Peak wished to discuss formal recruitment terms.

She thanked him and said she was still considering her options.

Cloudwalker Pavilion sent a woman with scholar's ink on her fingers who spoke about the theoretical underpinnings of the Stilled Surface Form with the enthusiasm of someone who had found a question she wanted to spend years answering. She noted that the structural understanding of metal-aligned qi expression was anomalous for Foundation level.

Baihua thanked her and said she was still considering.

Two more sects approached. Both were complimentary. Both were deferred.

Chen Mao was watching this from a short distance away. He had been selected by the Green Canopy Sect in the fourth round and was technically already committed. His expression was open amazement.

The Eternal Flame Order's approach did not come from a senior disciple.

Elder Shen walked across the field himself. Hands in his sleeves. His orange-red robes moved with the autumn wind. The other sect representatives watched him cross the field with the specific attention people gave to someone whose authority did not require assertion.

He stopped in front of her.

*"Well,"* he said.

*"Well,"* she said.

*"Six matches. Five of them against opponents above your listed stage."*

*"The brackets were drawn by lot."*

*"Mm."* He looked at her. *"The Iron Peak elder described your footwork as the most economical he has seen in forty years of recruitment observation. The Cloudwalker scholar is currently writing notes she will be thinking about for months."* A pause. *"I watched all six matches. What I observed was someone managing the distance between what they are and what they are willing to show. With considerable skill, and occasional creative editing."*

She said nothing.

*"The Eternal Flame Order has been waiting for something it could not name for a very long time,"* he said. *"I find, to my mild surprise, that I think we may have found it."* He tilted his head. *"What is your decision?"*

The Eternal Flame Order's pre-dynastic archives. Records of dimensional anomalies. Three hundred years of a man who had seen things no one else in this world had categories for.

*"The Eternal Flame Order,"* she said.

Something moved behind his eyes. Not surprise. Something quieter. Something she might have labeled satisfaction, or relief.

*"Good,"* he said. *"There is one additional matter."*

---

Chen Mao's expression when Elder Shen turned toward him was several emotions occurring simultaneously.

Surprise was uppermost. The surprise of being looked at by one of the most powerful cultivators in the empire with focused, intentional attention when you had been standing to the side and had assumed yourself scenery. Below the surprise: a reflexive attempt at composure, partially successful. Below that: the look of someone for whom the ground had shifted.

*"Chen Mao,"* Elder Shen said. *"Wood-aligned, fourth grade. You've already been selected by Green Canopy."*

*"I... yes, Elder."* His voice was steady. *"They offered formal recruitment in the fourth round."*

*"I know."* Elder Shen was quiet for a moment. *"The Eternal Flame Order does not often recruit wood-aligned disciples. Our focus lies elsewhere."* A pause, during which Chen Mao appeared to be bracing for a polite dismissal. *"However. We occasionally find value in things that don't fit our usual pattern."*

Chen Mao blinked.

*"Your growth technique in the demonstration round was genuinely good. Your third combat match showed adaptability under pressure that most candidates with your root grade don't demonstrate until their second or third year of formal training."* Elder Shen's manner was the same measured calm he brought to everything. *"More practically: you have already demonstrated a capacity to function effectively alongside"* (a brief, precise pause) *"unconventional circumstances. That is a useful quality."*

Chen Mao looked at Baihua. Baihua looked at the middle distance.

*"I am offering you a place in the Eternal Flame Order,"* Elder Shen said, *"if you choose to release your Green Canopy commitment. The choice is yours."*

Four seconds of silence.

*"Yes,"* Chen Mao said. *"I mean... yes. Elder. Thank you."*

Elder Shen nodded once and turned toward the gallery, where the other sect representatives were watching with varying degrees of displeasure. The Iron Peak elder's expression had moved through irritation into careful neutrality. The Cloudwalker scholar had stopped writing. The Green Canopy representative looked as though he had bitten into something unexpected.

No one said anything. Elder Shen was a Grand Elder of three centuries' standing. Whatever opinions existed about his recruitment choices existed quietly.

He walked back toward the gallery with his hands in his sleeves.

Chen Mao appeared at Baihua's elbow.

*"Did that..."* He stopped. Restarted. *"Did he just recruit me because of you?"*

*"Partially,"* she said.

*"That's..."* He processed this. *"Should I be offended?"*

*"Your technique demonstration was genuinely good,"* she said. *"He said so. He also noted your adaptability under pressure."*

*"But partly because of you."*

*"Partly."*

He was quiet for a moment. *"I've wanted to join a major sect since I was fifteen years old. I just got recruited by the Eternal Flame Order. By the Grand Elder personally."*

*"Yes."*

*"I think I'll be offended later,"* he decided. *"When there's more room for it."*

*"Reasonable,"* she said.

---

The bird arrived at the city's northern gate the following morning.

It was vast. Predominantly white, with primary feathers tipped in deep gold that caught the morning light. She estimated the wingspan at thirty meters fully extended. The eyes were the specific amber of very old intelligence. It landed outside the gate with the controlled precision of a creature that had done this many times, tucking its wings in a practiced motion, and regarded the assembled group with a calm that matched Elder Shen's own.

*"Tianming,"* Elder Shen said, by way of introduction. *"He has been with the Order for longer than most of our buildings."*

Tianming blinked one amber eye and made a sound between a resonant hum and a breath that Baihua's sensors registered as infrasonic.

*Qi saturation in the feathers: substantial. Possible demon core equivalent.*

The howdah strapped to Tianming's back was large enough for a dozen people, wood and lacquered leather, cushioned inside, with low railings along the open sides. The Eternal Flame Order's four new disciples boarded via a rope ladder.

Chen Mao's expression as he climbed and the ground dropped away below him cycled through awe, vertigo, and something that settled eventually on radiant disbelief. Xu Mingzhu settled into the rear bench with contained composure. Duan Ping sat down, gripped the railing once, and said nothing.

Baihua sat at the forward railing and watched the Vermillion Capital fall away below. The rooftops resolved into geometry, then into pattern, then into distance. She mapped it as it shrank, filing the final version alongside all her earlier versions.

Elder Shen settled beside her.

Below them, the Thousand Li Plains opened northward, vast and gold-green in the autumn light.

*"The sect is in the northern ranges,"* Elder Shen said. *"Three hours at Tianming's preferred pace. He dislikes being hurried."*

*"How old is he?"* she asked.

*"I stopped counting at two hundred years. He was already old when I met him."* A pause. *"He is the second oldest living thing I have encountered."*

She noted the implication.

*"You said your archives contain records of dimensional anomalies,"* she said.

*"They do."*

*"How many instances."*

*"Documented? Eleven, over four centuries. Suspected but unverified, perhaps twice that."* He looked out at the plains below. *"When something arrives from outside this world's dimensional framework, the disturbance propagates through the qi field, a pulse, a ripple, detectable at considerable distance by anyone specifically attuned to that frequency. I felt yours the night it happened. I did not know what had landed. I knew where, and I knew the magnitude was unlike anything in three centuries of records."* A pause. *"Most left little evidence. A scar in the earth. A residual energy signature that faded within years. Objects that didn't belong."*

*"Objects,"* she said.

*"Three times. Small things. Composed of materials our best artificers could not identify and could not affect. Heat, force, formation arrays, nothing worked on them."* He glanced at her. *"I imagine you may have encountered something similar recently."*

She said nothing.

*"The stone you carry,"* he said, *"has been generating a signal since you arrived in the Capital. Very faint. The kind of thing only someone specifically looking for dimensional resonance signatures would notice."*

She looked at him.

*"I have been looking for dimensional resonance signatures,"* he said, *"for a very long time."*

She ran the threat assessment in parallel with the conversation. A signal. Broadcasting. If Elder Shen could detect it, others of comparable sensitivity might also. The stone had been advertising her position since the cave. She considered shielding options (formation arrays, physical containment, nanobots reconfigured as a dampening field). None were viable without understanding the signal's mechanism. Proximity to Elder Shen and the Order's resources was a better strategy than concealment. But the signal was an open problem.

*"Why?"*

He was quiet for a moment. Below them, a river caught the light. Tianming's wingbeats were slow and enormous, each one a long glide, riding thermals.

*"Because,"* he said, *"forty years ago, something came through from elsewhere. Not an object. Not an energy signature."* He paused. *"A person. Or something shaped like one. It landed in the eastern ranges and was found by a patrol from our sect."*

Baihua was very still.

*"What happened to it?"*

*"It died,"* he said. *"Of the landing. It had been badly damaged in transit. Our healers couldn't help because nothing in its construction responded to qi-based medicine."* His voice was level, factual. *"We kept everything we could. It's in the archive."*

She looked at the mountains in the distance.

*"I'm telling you,"* he said, *"because you should know it's possible to not survive the journey. And because whatever you are looking for, you should know that others have looked for similar things, and not all of them found their way back."*

*"That one did not arrive intact,"* she said.

*"No."*

*"I did."*

*"Yes,"* he said. *"You did."* A pause. *"That is a meaningful difference."*

From behind them, Chen Mao's voice drifted forward, talking to Xu Mingzhu.

*"...I mean I knew the Order was old but I didn't know it was like, records-since-before-the-dynasty old, that's... how many books is that even..."*

*"Many,"* the girl said.

*"She's going to love the library,"* Elder Shen said, and Baihua registered that he meant her.

*"Yes,"* she said.

The mountains grew ahead of them, filling the northern horizon. Tianming rode a long thermal, tilting slightly, and the plains below shifted in the afternoon light.
