# PETAL OF THE VOID

## Chapter Thirteen: Eyes Above the World

---

The elder council meeting lasted four hours.

She sat at the outer edge of the chamber. Elder Shen had brought her in, and whatever Elder Zhu's private opinion was about an outer sect disciple attending a closed council session, the correspondence on the table made argument about procedural niceties seem wrong. The other elders (six of them, various stages, various tenure) had read the correspondence in sequence and arrived at the same place Elder Shen had arrived: a particular quality of silence that indicated people processing bad news they had already half-expected.

The discussion was thorough. The Iron Cauldron Sect's history. The timeline of the dragon operation. The probable composition of any force they might bring to bear. The Order's current defensive capabilities, which Elder Shen presented with an honesty that the council received with visible discomfort.

*"We have eleven inner sect disciples at Spirit Condensation stage or above,"* he said. *"Elder Zhu and myself at the higher levels. The northern passes engagement cost us two elders and four senior disciples. The Iron Cauldron Sect, if they are mobilizing as the correspondence suggests, will not come with fewer than several times our current combat-ready strength."*

She noted what he did not say: that his own Nascent Soul cultivation was still compromised. Her sensors read his meridian activity at roughly sixty percent of the output he had produced during the holding formation. Functional enough to chair a meeting and project authority. Not recovered enough to fight three Nascent Soul-stage opponents simultaneously. His right hand, when he wrote, still carried a fine tremor that the rest of the council pretended not to notice.

*"We send word to the allied sects,"* Elder Zhu said. *"Cloudwalker Pavilion, the Green Canopy School..."*

*"The correspondence suggests the timeline is imminent. Allied response takes weeks. We may not have weeks."*

The council looked at the table. Baihua looked at the council.

*"May I speak?"* she said.

Six faces turned toward her. Elder Shen's expression did not change.

*"The correspondence references three unknown elders at the Nascent Soul stage among the Iron Cauldron Sect's forces,"* she said. *"A mid-tier sect does not have three Nascent Soul elders through ordinary means. These are recruited assets, brought in specifically for this operation, almost certainly by the Architect. Which means they are mercenaries or coerced parties, not true believers. Their commitment to the Iron Cauldron Sect's cause is transactional."*

*"That's an optimistic interpretation,"* one of the other elders said.

*"It is a structural one. True believers fight until they can't. Transactional assets fight until the transaction becomes unprofitable."* She paused. *"I have an idea for how to address the three Nascent Soul elders specifically. I am not yet ready to detail it. What I need from the council is time, and I need a specific operational condition."*

*"Which is?"* Elder Zhu asked.

*"At some point in any engagement, I need the three Nascent Soul elders to be stationary, in close proximity to each other, for approximately two to three minutes."*

Silence.

*"That,"* Elder Zhu said, *"is a significant operational requirement."*

*"I know. I am working on how to create the condition. I am also working on something that will give us intelligence on their movements and composition that we currently don't have."*

*"What kind of intelligence?"*

*"Real-time. Comprehensive. Covering a significant geographic area."* She paused. *"I need a location away from the sect. Somewhere remote. And I need Elder Shen's authorization for an extended absence from outer sect training obligations."*

The council looked at Elder Shen.

*"How long?"* he said.

*"Four weeks. Possibly five."*

Another silence.

*"Done,"* he said.

---

She found the location two days later.

The high plateau three li northeast of the sect's mountain, above the treeline, accessible via a route she mapped in half a day. Flat basalt. Clear sky. The nearest human habitation was several li in any direction.

She began transporting materials on the third day.

---

The problem she had set herself was, by any reasonable measure, extraordinary.

She needed to place observation platforms in a stable orbit above this world. Platforms small enough to be undetectable by the world's existing sensing methods, capable of sustained operation on available power sources, and able to transmit data back to ground-based receivers she could build and operate.

The engineering challenge broke into four components.

First: launch vehicle. She needed a mechanism to accelerate a payload to orbital velocity (approximately eight kilometers per second at the equatorial orbital altitude she was targeting). Chemical propulsion was not feasible with available materials. The alternative: formation-based thrust. She had spent two evenings at the sect running calculations, working through whether the principles she understood from her home dimension translated to this world's physics. They did, with modifications. Formation arrays could generate directed force. The impedance-matched arrays she had been developing were significantly more efficient than standard formations. A staged vehicle with multiple formation-array thrust units (each powered by a cluster of high-grade spirit stones, designed to burn completely before the next stage ignited) could in principle achieve the delta-v she needed.

The spirit stone consumption would be substantial. She calculated it against her auction proceeds and found it feasible. Just.

Second: orbital platforms. Small, formation-powered, equipped with optical sensors she would build herself (the sensor design from the archive scout was a proven platform she could modify). The challenge was power: orbital platforms couldn't be recharged from the ground. The answer: small qi-collection formation arrays on the platform's exterior, drawing on the ambient qi field that permeated the entire world. The qi field didn't diminish at altitude (the cultivation texts' cosmological sections were clear on this, qi being a property of the world itself rather than its surface). The platforms would harvest ambient qi indefinitely.

Third: data transmission. She had built a qi-thread connection for the archive scout (short-range, high-fidelity). Orbital-range transmission required a different approach: lower bandwidth, higher power, using the qi field as a propagation medium. She spent a week developing the transmission protocol from first principles, testing it with prototype transceivers at progressively larger distances across the plateau.

Fourth: ground infrastructure. Receivers at the sect, disguised as formation array components. A processing system (another prototype, the most complex she had yet built) that would receive the transmitted data and render it into a readable format. And drones: six smaller vehicles, atmosphere-bound, carrying high-resolution optical sensors, each controllable through a qi-thread link.

She built all of it.

---

The plateau became her workshop. No one to explain things to. No sessions to attend. No outer sect obligations structuring her time. Only the work and the sky and the steady rhythm of construction.

She built the launch vehicle in stages, each stage a separate assembly tested independently before integration. The formation-array thrust units were the most technically demanding component. She was operating at the edge of what the ancient artificers' notes described as achievable, pushing the impedance-matching principles further than she had taken them in the auction innovations. She rebuilt the first thrust unit three times before it produced the specific output she needed.

The spirit stones diminished steadily as she tested and refined. The margin was sufficient but not comfortable. She worked more carefully as the reserves decreased.

The orbital platforms she built in a sequence of four, each one the size of her open palm, each carrying a miniaturized optical sensor array, a qi-collection formation, a data transmission unit, and a positioning system derived from the formation arrays' capacity to maintain fixed spatial relationships. Four platforms in two complementary orbital planes would give her continuous coverage of a significant geographic area.

The drone design was simpler (she had been building small formation-powered flyers since the archive scout). Six of them, each carrying an optical sensor, each formation-powered and controllable through a qi-thread link to the ground processing system.

She worked for thirty-two days.

On the thirty-third day, she stood at the center of the plateau in the pre-dawn dark and looked at the launch vehicle.

It was taller than she was. Not by much, but enough. Three stages, each a cylinder of refined materials she had worked using methods unavailable to the world's blacksmiths, the formation arrays etched into their surfaces with precision she had developed over months of artificer work. At the top: four orbital platforms, folded into a deployment configuration that would open once the vehicle reached its target altitude.

She ran her final checks.

*Launch vehicle: nominal. Deployment mechanism: nominal. Platform systems: nominal. Ground receiver: nominal. Transmission protocol: verified at maximum tested range.*

She stepped back to the designated safe distance.

She activated the first stage.

---

The first-stage formation arrays ignited simultaneously, not with fire (formation thrust didn't produce combustion) but with a sustained brilliant white output that lit the plateau. The sound was not an explosion but a deep resonant hum that she felt through the basalt beneath her feet. Three seconds of build, and then the vehicle was moving, accelerating upward, compressing into a streak of white light against the pre-dawn sky.

The first stage burned for forty seconds. She tracked the vehicle through her sensors, watching the altitude climb.

At forty seconds, the first stage separated. The second stage ignited, a smaller light, higher, moving faster. She tracked it until it was beyond visual range, then continued tracking through her sensor array's long-distance capability until that reached its limit.

She switched to the transmission receiver.

Six minutes. Nothing. The vehicle was above atmosphere, above the reach of anything in this world.

Then the first handshake signal came through.

*Platform One: online. Orbital insertion: successful. Qi-collection array: nominal. Transmission: established.*

Three more handshakes followed at intervals as the remaining platforms deployed and established their orbits. By the time the sun cleared the eastern horizon, all four were operational.

She looked up at the sky, clear and pale and ordinary in appearance. Somewhere above it, four small objects moved in their silent orbits, looking down at everything.

*"Good,"* she said.

---

She spent four days calibrating the system and building the drones.

The ground data from the orbital platforms resolved the world below in detail she found simultaneously useful and disorienting. The Eternal Flame Order's mountain was identifiable by its formation array signature. The Vermillion Capital was a concentration of life-energy so dense it appeared as a distinct node. The northern passes showed the fading signature of the dragon's absence.

And to the southeast, she found what she was looking for.

---

The Iron Cauldron Sect's mountain was larger than the Eternal Flame Order's.

She studied it for two days through the platform data, building a picture of its layout and activity patterns. Then she deployed three drones, sent them on a course that kept them at altitude until they reached the region, then descended for close observation.

What she found was not comforting.

The training grounds were active in a way that had nothing to do with ordinary sect operations. The movement patterns were not the patterns of disciples training. They were the patterns of force assembly. Cultivators arriving from multiple directions, from affiliations she identified through qi signatures and confirmed through the drones' visual resolution. Not just Iron Cauldron disciples. A coalition assembled under one banner.

She counted. She recounted. She let the drones orbit for a full day to ensure a representative picture.

The total assembled force was approximately three hundred cultivators. Roughly two hundred at Foundation stage, sixty at Spirit Condensation, and the remainder (the ones who moved differently, who occupied the best positions, who the others gave space to) at Core Formation or above.

Set apart from the rest, housed in a separate compound: three individuals she tracked with particular attention. Their qi signatures were the largest in the facility. She compared them against her calibration point (Elder Shen) and found readings consistent with the cultivation texts' descriptions of Nascent Soul stage.

Three Nascent Soul cultivators. Unknown affiliation. Arrived within the past two weeks. Housed separately. Given deference by everyone.

She recorded everything and flew the drones home.

---

She returned to the sect on the thirty-eighth day, carrying the ground receiver units and the drone control system. She had left the plateau clean, the stone scoured by the vehicle's thrust output and then by two days of wind.

Elder Shen convened the council that evening.

She set up the processing system in the council chamber, a formation array board designed to present orbital platform data in a form that cultivators could interpret. The output was a map: moving, updating, real-time, showing the Iron Cauldron Sect's compound and its surroundings, with markers indicating the position and estimated cultivation level of every occupant.

The council looked at it. Several of them looked at her. Several of them looked back at the map.

*"How,"* Elder Zhu said.

*"I placed observation platforms at very high altitude,"* Baihua said. *"Higher than any cultivator has ever flown. They transmit data to the receiver here through the qi field. The drones provide close-range visual detail."*

*"How high is very high?"*

*"High enough that the world's curvature is visible. High enough that the platforms can observe the entire region simultaneously."* She paused. *"This world has no sensing method that operates at that altitude. The platforms are invisible to any detection method available here."*

*"You built this,"* one of the elders said, *"in thirty-eight days."*

*"I had the relevant knowledge and the materials. The primary constraint was time, not capability."*

She let them look at the map.

*"Three hundred cultivators,"* Elder Shen said.

*"Approximately. The number changes slightly as small groups arrive or rotate. The trend is upward."*

*"And three Nascent Soul assets."*

*"Yes. All three are consistently housed in the separate compound. They move through the main facility at irregular intervals, for meals and occasional assessment of the assembled forces."* She paused. *"Their movement patterns have one consistent feature. Once per day, in the late afternoon, all three are in the same location simultaneously. The elevated terrace on the compound's eastern side. For approximately thirty minutes, based on the past week of observation."*

The council looked at her.

*"Thirty minutes,"* Elder Shen said.

*"Yes."*

*"You mentioned in the previous meeting that you needed them stationary, in proximity, for two to three minutes."*

*"Yes."*

*"And you have identified a window of thirty minutes per day in which that condition is met."*

*"Yes."*

*"And you still won't tell us what you're planning."*

*"Not yet. The method requires a preparation step I have not yet completed."* She paused. *"What I can tell you is that if the condition is met, the three Nascent Soul assets will be removed from the engagement. The remaining forces (three hundred cultivators at Foundation to Core Formation) are a serious threat but a manageable one with allied support, reinforced defenses, and the sect's full remaining strength."*

*"The allied sects,"* Elder Zhu said. *"We've sent word. Cloudwalker Pavilion has confirmed they will send support. Green Canopy School as well. Estimated arrival: three weeks."*

*"The platform data suggests the Iron Cauldron Sect's assembly will be complete in approximately two weeks,"* Baihua said. *"The mobilization pattern is consistent with a force preparing to move, not a force preparing to wait."*

Two weeks until the Iron Cauldron Sect was ready. Three weeks until allied support arrived. One week was the gap they needed to close.

*"Which is why I need to complete the preparation step before they move,"* she said.

*"What do you need?"*

*"Access to the sub-level archive. The Voidheart Stone deposit that Brother Wuming identified beneath the library. I need to study it."*

Elder Shen looked at her for a long moment.

*"Done,"* he said.

*"And I need the council to trust me on the method,"* she said. *"I understand that is a significant ask. I would not make it if I had a better option."*

Elder Zhu looked at the map, at the three markers in the separate compound, at the daily thirty-minute window the platforms had been recording for a week.

*"You've already been right about everything,"* Elder Zhu said. *"The correspondence. The observation platforms. The timeline."* A pause. *"Don't stop being right."*

*"I have no current plans to begin being wrong,"* Baihua said. This produced a brief silence in the chamber that suggested it had landed differently than she had intended.

*"Good enough,"* Elder Zhu said.

---

She found Chen Mao afterward. He was standing outside the council chamber door with his arms folded.

He had been asked to wait outside this time (Elder Zhu's decision, delivered with the quiet firmness of someone who had tolerated one breach of protocol in the first meeting and did not intend to tolerate a second). He had accepted this with the grace of someone who understood the difference between being excluded from a room and being excluded from what mattered.

*"Well?"* he said.

She looked at him. She had been thinking, on the walk from the plateau, about what to tell him.

*"The Iron Cauldron Sect is moving in two weeks,"* she said. *"Allied support arrives in three. I have a method to address the largest threat in their force, but I need to complete a preparation step first."*

He absorbed this. *"Those things you placed in the sky,"* he said. *"The ones that give you the map."*

She looked at him. *"How do you know about those?"*

*"I don't know what they are,"* he said. *"But you disappeared to a plateau for thirty-eight days and came back with a formation board that shows a real-time map of another sect's compound. Fen Liuying said it looked like something that could only be done by someone who could see from a height no cultivator could reach."* He paused. *"She said it very quietly and then looked at the ceiling for a while."*

*"Fen Liuying is perceptive,"* Baihua said.

*"She is."* He looked at her. *"The three Nascent Soul elders. That's what you're planning to deal with."*

*"Yes."*

*"How?"*

*"I'm not ready to explain it yet."*

He was quiet for a moment. *"Your cultivation form progress."*

*"I know,"* she said. *"How is it going?"*

The corner of his mouth moved. *"The shoulder junction holds for about fifteen seconds now before I lose it. Cui Hao says my output has visibly improved."*

*"Good,"* she said. *"Keep going."*

*"I will."* He looked at her. *"Be careful."*

*"I will be precise,"* she said, *"which is better."*

He looked at her for a moment longer with the expression she had been learning, slowly, to read rather than merely catalogue.

*"I know,"* he said. *"I know it's better. I still want you to be careful too."*

She looked at him. She went to find Brother Wuming.

---

The violet lamp burned at his desk. He looked up when she entered (which he did not always do).

*"The sub-level,"* she said. *"I need the Voidheart deposit."*

*"Yes,"* he said. *"I know."* He produced a key from beneath the desk, a physical key, old iron. *"The sub-level has a secondary entrance below the archive chamber. The Voidheart formation is in the northern wall, approximately three meters down from the ceiling. You will recognize it."*

She took the key. *"I will begin tonight."*

*"I know,"* he said again. Then, after she had turned to go: *"Baihua."*

She paused.

*"Whatever you are planning for the three elders,"* he said, *"the Voidheart Stone will help. It is not only useful for what we discussed. Its dimensional properties affect more than just formation binding."* A pause. *"I thought you should know that. In case it is relevant to your preparation."*

She looked at him. He looked back, and she had the sense she sometimes had with Brother Wuming, that he knew more about what she was planning than she had told him.

*"Thank you,"* she said.

*"Come back,"* he said. *"With all the pieces."*

She went down to the sub-level.
