# PETAL OF THE VOID

## Chapter Three: What Burns at the Center of Things

---

She left Silverwind City before dawn.

The gate guards were different from the morning shift, two men sitting on stools inside the gatehouse with a lamp between them, playing a tile game she did not recognize. One of them looked up when she passed. He looked at the robe, looked at her face, looked back at his tiles.

She walked through the gate and onto the north road.

Her optical array functioned across the full spectrum regardless of ambient light. The road was clear in her vision: packed earth, cart ruts, the impressions of many feet, running northeast along the edge of the Jade Canopy Mountains before cutting across the lower foothills. From there, the map she had built from the library's geographical scrolls showed open flatland to the Vermillion Capital.

She walked for six hours.

The terrain was uneventful. The road followed the mountain edge, rising and falling with the contours of the foothills, crossing two shallow streams on stone bridges and passing through three small settlements (clusters of houses, smoke from morning cook-fires, no one on the road at this hour). She maintained a steady pace and catalogued the environment as she went, building her baseline readings of the ambient qi field with each kilometer.

At approximately noon, with the sun directly overhead and the road running straight through a section of thinning forest, her sensors registered something to the east.

She stopped walking.

The reading was subtle. A faint electromagnetic irregularity, layered beneath the standard qi-field saturation she had been measuring for two days. She had built her baseline carefully, and she knew the signature of ambient qi well enough to recognize when something was threaded through it that did not belong. This signal was denser than ambient qi. It had structure, the kind of pattern that suggested organized information rather than background noise.

She stood at the roadside for eleven seconds, weighing the deviation against her timeline to the Capital.

She stepped off the road and into the trees.

---

The forest east of the merchant road was older than the forest she had walked through on her first night. The trees here were larger, more widely spaced, their canopies high enough that the undergrowth beneath them had thinned to almost nothing. Mist pooled between the roots in patches. The quality of silence was different here, denser, heavier. Her sensors registered the qi-field density at approximately three times the ambient level of the surrounding region.

*Naturally occurring spiritual nexus.* She drew the classification from the cultivation texts. Locations where qi concentrated due to geological or topographical factors. Associated with monster habitation.

She revised that classification's practical significance when she smelled the creatures.

A sharp musk, underlaid with something chemical. Acid, imperfectly contained. She stopped and scanned.

Four of them. They emerged from the mist between the trees in a loose semicircle, approaching from three directions. Roughly wolf-shaped but wrong in the proportions: shoulders too high, skulls flattened, eyes a uniform reflective amber with no visible pupil. Each stood approximately 1.3 meters at the shoulder. Their paws left impressions in the soft soil that steamed faintly as the morning air made contact.

*Acid-fang wolves. Low-tier monster. Pack hunters. Corrosive saliva.*

They spread further apart as they approached, the two on the flanks moving wider, the two in the center holding position. Coordinated flanking behavior.

The nearest one lunged.

She sidestepped by the exact amount required to clear its jaws and caught it by the scruff of the neck as it passed. Its momentum was considerable for its mass. She redirected that momentum into the trunk of the nearest pine. The wolf hit the trunk at speed. The impact sound was sharp and the tree shook, dropping needles. The wolf dropped to the ground and did not immediately rise.

The second came from her left, faster than the first, its jaw open, saliva trailing from the fangs in thin strands. She turned into its charge and took the full weight of it against her left forearm. The saliva hit the ceramic plating and sizzled. She noted *minor surface degradation, non-structural* and drove her elbow down across the back of the wolf's neck. The cervical vertebrae separated under the impact. The wolf folded.

The third and fourth pulled up short. They stood four meters away, their amber eyes on her, their bodies low. Four seconds passed. The wolves looked at each other. Then they turned and ran, disappearing back into the mist.

She watched them go. She checked the forearm plating. The acid had etched a faint pattern into the ceramic surface, a series of thin lines where the saliva had pooled. Non-structural damage.

She continued walking east. The signal was stronger now.

---

Two hours deeper into the old forest. The qi density thickened around her steadily, requiring her sensors to recalibrate their range three times. The trees gave way to a limestone escarpment, a cliff face approximately thirty meters high, its surface draped in ferns and trailing moss. At the base of the escarpment, half-hidden behind a curtain of hanging vegetation, a cave mouth opened into the rock.

The signal was coming from inside.

She pulled the vegetation aside. The cave passage was narrow for two meters, the limestone walls close on both sides, the ceiling low enough that she could have touched it. Then it widened.

The chamber beyond was roughly twenty meters across and eight meters high. The walls were limestone, shot through with veins of a crystalline material that caught the faint light from the entrance and scattered it in cold patterns across the floor. The air was cooler here than outside, and still.

At the far end of the chamber, on a natural shelf of rock worn smooth (by water, she estimated, over a period of centuries), sat a stone.

It was not large. Roughly the size of her closed fist, irregular in shape, a deep matte grey. It pulsed with a faint internal luminescence at slow, irregular intervals. She swept it with her full sensor array.

*Composition: unknown. Energy signature: anomalous. Classification: unable to process.*

She took two steps toward it.

Something moved in the dark at the back of the chamber.

---

It uncoiled slowly.

She registered the movement first as a shift in the thermal map of the cave's rear wall, a large heat source changing shape. Then her optical sensors resolved the details. Scales, deep iridescent green-black, their surface absorbing light rather than reflecting it. A body, serpentine, massive, pulling itself upward from a coiled resting position with the controlled deliberation of something that had not needed to hurry for a very long time.

The head came up last. It was the size of a large cart. The fangs, visible as the mouth opened slightly, were each the length of her forearm. The eyes were old amber, and they were not the eyes of an animal. There was something behind them, a pressure, a density, a quality of attention that her sensors flagged with a tag she had not used since reading the cultivation texts.

*Demon core detected. Estimated stage: early Core Formation equivalent.*

She estimated the body at twelve meters from head to tail. Mass: approximately four tonnes. The demon core reading meant this creature had been cultivating qi naturally for centuries, possibly millennia, compressing it into an inner core the same way human cultivators did. It was no longer simply a large serpent. It was something that had crossed a threshold.

The serpent regarded her with those amber eyes. Then it opened its mouth fully. The interior was deep red. Something dark and viscous beaded at the tips of the fangs. It made a sound, not a hiss (lower than a hiss, more resonant), that she felt through the cave floor and up through her feet and into her chassis. A subsonic warning. The oldest kind of communication: *leave*.

She looked at the stone on the shelf behind the serpent.

She looked at the serpent.

*"I require the object behind you,"* she said, in the language of this world.

The serpent struck.

---

The head came down fast. Faster than the acid wolves, faster than anything she had encountered in this world. She threw herself sideways with every bit of speed her locomotion systems could produce and the fangs still caught her across the left shoulder, shearing through the outer silk robe and scoring two deep gouges across the ceramic armour beneath. The impact transmitted through her chassis with enough force to register on every sensor in her left side. Her shoulder assembly rang with the shock of it.

She hit the cave floor, rolled, came upright.

*Structural assessment: left shoulder armour compromised. Two gouges, depth 4mm, ceramic integrity reduced. Silk robe: torn. Functional capacity: unimpaired.*

The serpent was already repositioning. Its body shifted in the confined space with the controlled fluidity of something that knew this cave and had fought in it before. The amber eyes tracked her. No hurry in them. No alarm.

She needed data.

She crossed the distance in a burst of speed and drove her fist into the serpent's midsection, directly above the fourth coil, with the full output of her standard locomotion power. The impact sent a visible shockwave through the scales. The serpent recoiled, its body pulling back from the strike point, and she registered the recoil as confirmation of damage.

Then the tail came around.

It swung in a lateral arc, low, covering the full width of the cave behind her. She saw it coming and could not fully avoid it in the confined space. The tail connected with her midsection and the force of four tonnes of animated muscle drove her backward into the cave wall.

The limestone cracked behind her. She slid down the wall to a seated position and took stock.

*Left shoulder armour: compromised. Midsection impact: significant force transfer. Structural integrity: revised to 84%.*

*Down from 91% at the crater.* The crash had cost her nine percent. The serpent was costing her the rest of the margin.

The serpent was already repositioning again, the amber eyes tracking her with that same steady intelligence. It knew she was dangerous. It was not afraid. It was assessing.

She could continue this. Hit it again, absorb another tail strike, gradually wear it down through repeated impacts. She would win. But winning would take time and the cave was a poor environment for a sustained engagement, and each exchange was pushing her structural integrity further into a range where additional engagements would become constrained.

She made a decision.

---

She straightened up from the wall and went still.

Not the stillness of bracing for impact. A different quality of stillness. Complete. Deliberate. The stillness of something that has stopped managing itself.

She reached inward.

The fusion core sat in its housing. Patient. Cycling through its reactions. It did not know urgency. It did not know threat. It burned because that was what it did.

She had always kept a regulator between herself and the core's full output, a throttle, a limiter. She could not remember when this had begun, only that the setting was deep and embedded, older than her current awareness. Some buried system architecture she couldn't fully access held it in place.

She reached past the regulator, found the threshold, and pushed.

The response was immediate.

Heat bloomed outward from her chest. Not gradual, not directional. Everywhere at once. The air around her temperature-shifted so rapidly that the cave's ambient moisture flash-vaporized into steam. Dense white clouds poured off her shoulders, her arms, her hands. Her thermal management systems engaged at emergency capacity, every cooling channel in her chassis running at full output, venting heat through her skin.

She looked at her hands.

She was glowing.

A deep amber-gold radiance, pouring from the micro-vents in her chassis and through the cracks in her armour and through her eyes. She could see it reflected in the serpent's scales. The cave walls around her were warming visibly, the crystalline veins in the limestone fluorescing in response to the radiant output.

*Thermal threshold: 67%. 71%. 74%.*

The steam kept coming, filling the cave in dense rolling clouds. The serpent's outline wavered through the haze.

The serpent had stopped moving.

It was watching her. The quality of attention in the amber eyes had changed. The steady intelligence was still there, but something else had joined it. Recognition. She read it in the way the head pulled back slightly, the way the coils tightened, the specific shift in the creature's posture that she classified as the response of a being that had spent centuries in a spiritual nexus and had, in all that time, sensed something like the energy she was now producing. Not in anything that walked on two legs. In the deeper structures of the world. In the ground. In the sky.

*"Yes,"* she said. *"Now you understand."*

She raised her right hand, opened her palm, and released.

---

What came out was not fire. Fire was combustion, a chemical process, rapid oxidation, fuel consumed. What poured from her palm was the output of a fusion reaction, pressure and mass past a critical threshold, hydrogen converted to helium, energy that had powered stars.

It was a column of concentrated stellar output approximately forty centimeters in diameter.

It lasted for 0.8 seconds.

When it stopped, the serpent was gone. The shelf of rock at the back of the cave was gone. Approximately eleven meters of cave wall was gone. In its place was a rough circular aperture, four meters across, opening onto the forest beyond. At the far edge of the clearing the blast had created (approximately fifteen meters of trees simply absent), other trees were burning at their tops where secondary radiation had caught the canopy.

She stood in the settling steam and ran her diagnostics.

*Thermal threshold: 91%. Emergency cooling: active. Core output: returning to nominal.*

She looked at the burning trees through the aperture. She looked at the hole in the cave wall. She looked at the space where the serpent had been and found nothing. No remains. No fragments. The blast had been comprehensive.

*Calibration required. That was excessive.*

She engaged her cooling systems at full capacity and stood still for ninety seconds, letting the thermal load dissipate. The steam rose off her skin in thinning curls. The amber-gold radiance faded as the core throttled back to its regulated state. The cave around her ticked and groaned, the stone re-settling after the thermal shock.

Outside, the fire in the canopy was spreading. She needed to finish and leave.

When the glow had faded enough that she was no longer radiating visible light, she walked to the rock shelf, or what remained of it, and looked for the stone.

---

It was still there.

Everything else within the blast radius was either absent or significantly altered. The stone sat in a shallow depression in the surviving lip of the shelf, undamaged, still pulsing with that slow irregular inner light.

She reached out and picked it up.

When her fingers closed around it, something happened.

Not pain. Not warmth, though the surface was warm. Not sound, though her auditory array registered a faint sustained frequency she could not source. What she felt was closer to resonance. The stone's pulse and her core's pulse, the two of them finding each other across the distance between her palm and her chest, testing the relationship between them.

Her sensors swept it again. *Composition: unknown. Energy signature: anomalous.* But this time there was an additional line at the bottom of the report, generated by a sub-system she had not consciously queried, surfacing its conclusion:

*Possible classification: extra-dimensional artifact.*

She looked at that line for a long time.

She looked at the stone in her palm. At its slow, uncertain light. At the way it pulsed, not with the steady rhythm of a heartbeat or a reactor cycle, but with the irregular cadence of something searching. A signal, broadcasting into silence, that had perhaps just received something back.

She turned it over. Scanned it from every angle. Cross-referenced its energy signature against every reading she had taken since arriving in this world. No match. Cross-referenced it against the partial records in her own corrupted memory archive.

Static. White noise. An error message where the comparison result should have been.

She had insufficient data.

She wrapped the stone in the remnant of her silk sash (torn at the shoulder from the serpent's strike) and tucked it against her chest, behind the outer robe, as close to her core as the armour would allow. The pulsing continued. She did not expect it to stop.

The fire in the canopy outside was growing. Smoke was beginning to drift into the cave through the aperture.

She stepped through the hole in the cave wall, oriented herself northeast, and began walking toward the Vermillion Capital.

Behind her, the trees burned.
