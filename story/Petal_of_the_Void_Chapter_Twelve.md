# PETAL OF THE VOID

## Chapter Twelve: The Price of Things

---

She spent her first day in the Vermillion Capital doing what she had done the first time: walking in expanding spirals, building the current map against the map she had filed months ago, noting what had changed and what hadn't.

Most things hadn't. The same amber glow above the walls at dusk. The same market district density, the same temple clusters trailing incense smoke. The imperial district still rose in tiered terraces to the north. The sect pavilions from the Recruitment were gone, replaced by ordinary commerce. The examination grounds were empty.

She noted the Vermillion Autumn Auction house on her second pass through the commerce district, a building of dark stone set slightly back from the main avenue, its signage understated. She noted its entrance guards (four, Spirit Condensation stage minimum), its delivery entrance on the side street (two guards, same level), and the formation arrays woven into the building's facade (a combination of security and qi-dampening measures).

She presented Elder Shen's letter of introduction at the front desk the following morning.

The reception clerk read it, looked at her, read it again, and disappeared into a back office. He returned with a senior representative, a middle-aged woman with the specific polish of someone whose professional existence involved making wealthy people comfortable. She read the letter, looked at Baihua, and said: *"Elder Shen speaks very highly of your capabilities. We would be delighted to facilitate your participation in the auction."*

*"I also have items to consign,"* Baihua said.

The representative's expression shifted into attentiveness. *"Of course. If you would follow me."*

---

The consignment assessment took three hours.

She had brought six items from the sect, the products of weeks of evening work in Sister Chenfa's workshop, set aside because she had known since reading the artificer texts that this moment would come.

The first item: a formation array inscribed on a palm-sized jade disk that, when activated with minimal qi input, produced a sustained temperature-regulation effect in an enclosed space. Standard jade, standard formation ink, standard materials, but the array design incorporated the impedance-matching principles she had introduced to Master Qin, which meant it ran at approximately forty percent of the spirit stone consumption of equivalent commercial products while producing the same output. She had made three of them.

The representative turned one over in her hands. She activated it with a touch of qi and watched the air above it shimmer. *"The efficiency is..."*

*"Approximately forty percent improved over current market equivalents,"* Baihua said. *"The formation design is proprietary. The array cannot be easily reverse-engineered."*

The representative set it down carefully. *"And the others?"*

The second item: two compact formation array boards designed for qi-thread communication over distances up to fifty li, with improved signal clarity over standard communication formations.

The third item: a formation-enhanced storage vessel, a lacquered box with internal formation arrays that maintained a stable qi-neutral environment. The innovation was a self-regulating feedback loop that prevented the internal qi environment from drifting over time. Current equivalents required periodic recalibration. This one did not.

The fourth, fifth, and sixth items: formation tools (styluses, etching guides, calibration instruments) designed with ergonomic geometry she had derived from observing how human artificers held and moved their tools, incorporating minor formation enhancements to improve precision during delicate array work.

The representative spent a long time with the sixth item.

*"Where did you train?"* she asked.

*"The Eternal Flame Order,"* Baihua said. *"Outer sect, first year."*

The representative looked at her. *"First year."*

*"Yes."*

A pause. *"These will sell,"* the representative said. *"Particularly the temperature arrays and the communication boards. I would recommend not setting a reserve price. Let the room determine value."*

*"I defer to your expertise."*

*"There are two other items I would like to acquire,"* she added. *"A specimen of Voidheart Stone listed in the auction catalogue. And Celestial Iron."*

The representative looked at the sourcing document entry Baihua indicated. *"The Celestial Iron block. Yes. It's been with us for two seasons. The smithing houses have all assessed it and declined. The hardness is beyond current working methods."* She paused. *"Most buyers at that level want material they can use."*

*"The demand from me is not limited,"* Baihua said.

---

The auction opened on the third day.

The hall was large and tiered. Private booths ran along the upper tier for those who preferred anonymity. The open floor below was arranged in curved rows facing the auction platform. She sat in the open floor (she had considered a private booth and rejected it; the booths provided anonymity but limited her sensory access to the room, and sensory access was more valuable).

Her consignment items came up in the first quarter of the auction, grouped together as *artificer innovations from an Eternal Flame Order disciple*.

The bidding on the temperature arrays opened at thirty spirit stones. It reached sixty in the first minute. It reached one hundred by the time it closed, three items total. The buyers moved with the energy of people who had acquired something they intended to profit from.

The communication boards generated similar intensity. Several upper-tier booths entered the bidding. Final price: one hundred and ninety spirit stones for the pair.

The storage vessel went to a medicinal sect representative for eighty stones.

The artificer tools sold individually to three separate buyers, each driven by genuine professional recognition. Combined: ninety stones.

She sat with her hands folded in her lap and watched four hundred and sixty spirit stones materialize from objects she had built in the evenings of three weeks, from materials that had cost her eleven stones in total, from principles that had been sitting undiscovered in the intersection between her home dimension's physics and this world's cultivation theory.

She set aside the amount she calculated for the Celestial Iron. The remainder she held in reserve for the Voidheart Stone.

---

The Celestial Iron came up two hours into the auction.

A block approximately thirty centimeters on each side, dark silver-grey, with a surface that seemed to absorb light rather than reflect it. The auctioneer described its provenance (deep mountain excavation, eastern range), its assessed hardness (beyond measurement), and its limitation (no current blacksmithing method could work it).

The bidding was modest. Most of the room had already assessed it. A few bids came from collectors and curiosity buyers. The price settled at sixty-five stones. She bid sixty-eight. A counter-bid at seventy. She bid seventy-two. The counter-bidder reconsidered the material's limitations and declined.

She had the Celestial Iron at seventy-two stones.

---

The Voidheart Stone was the final major lot.

It was presented with more ceremony than anything else, brought out under its own carrying formation. A dark grey stone roughly forty centimeters across, irregular in shape, with an internal luminescence that pulsed at slow intervals. She recognized the pulse immediately.

Her sensors swept it from across the room. *Composition: complex mineral matrix with dimensional qi integration. Energy signature: anomalous. Resonance properties: exceptional. Classification: Voidheart Stone, high-purity specimen.*

Her extra-dimensional stone pulsed once against her chest.

The auctioneer named an opening bid higher than anything else in the catalogue.

Twelve hands went up.

---

The bidding was not orderly.

She had expected competition. She had not anticipated its specific character: the way certain buyers moved, the way sect insignia appeared on people she had not catalogued on entry, the way some bids were placed not with competitive decisiveness but with studied deliberateness intended to communicate that withdrawing was the sensible choice.

She catalogued the room as the bidding progressed.

Seven serious bidders after the first round. One Iron Peak Sect (mechanical consistency, predetermined budget). Two wealthy independent cultivators in private booths. One trading house representative.

And three she identified (from their bearing, their positioning, and the way they distributed their attention across the room rather than the platform) as something other than ordinary buyers. They were sitting in three separate locations in the open floor with no visible connection between them. But she had been cataloguing threat postures since the first morning she had walked out of a crater, and she knew coordinated positioning when she saw it.

*Sect insignia: partially concealed. Movement style: trained combat practitioners. Distribution: triangulated around the auction floor.*

She bid.

One of the three looked at her. The look was not competitive. It was the look of someone noting a complication. She looked back with the complete absence of expression that was simply her default state. The man looked away first.

The bidding continued. The Iron Peak representative reached their ceiling and withdrew. One independent cultivator withdrew. The trading house representative stayed in. The three coordinated bidders stayed in.

The price climbed past her allocation. She revised her allocation. The price climbed past the revision. She revised again. Each time one of the three bidders placed a number, they looked at her. Not at the platform. At her. The message was not subtle: *this is our lot and you should understand that.*

She bid.

At a price that made the auctioneer's voice carry genuine excitement, the trading house representative withdrew. The three coordinated bidders paused. She could see them recalibrating, some silent communication passing between them.

One of them turned in his seat and looked directly at her. Older than the other two, long face, eyes that had the quality of someone who had done violence many times and found it unremarkable. He held up two fingers.

She bid again.

They exchanged looks. A longer pause. The auctioneer prompted. The older one shook his head. They withdrew.

The gavel came down.

She had the Voidheart Stone. Three hundred and twelve spirit stones. Nearly everything that remained of her auction proceeds after the Celestial Iron. The launch vehicle's spirit stone requirements would need to be met from the remainder, and the remainder was thin.

She would make it work.

---

She collected her lots from the claims office (the Voidheart Stone in a protective formation case, the Celestial Iron in a reinforced crate). She arranged for spirit stone transfer and left through the main entrance into the late afternoon street.

She had gone approximately forty meters when her sensors flagged movement on three vectors simultaneously.

Seven combatants. Two ahead at the next junction, emerging from a side street. Three behind, following at distance. Two above, roof positions, left and right. Cultivation bases ranging from Spirit Condensation to early Core Formation. The three coordinated bidders from the auction floor, plus four additional assets positioned before the auction ended.

Pre-planned ambush. They had identified her as the likely winning bidder and positioned in advance.

She stopped walking.

The two ahead emerged fully from the side street. The street had emptied, ordinary citizens removing themselves from the vicinity. One of the two was the older man from the auction, the one who had given her the two-finger warning.

*"The Stone and the Iron,"* he said. *"Set them down. Walk away. You'll keep your life."*

She looked at him. She looked at the roof positions. She looked at the three behind her without turning, tracking them through her sensor array.

*Seven combatants, coordinated, pre-positioned, Core Formation presence. Against an ordinary Spirit Condensation or Foundation cultivator, this would be effectively terminal.*

*"No,"* she said.

The older man moved first, a fast draw of qi-enhanced speed, crossing the distance with the confidence of someone who had done this to better cultivators than this apparent Foundation-level girl. She sidestepped by the exact amount required, took his extended arm at the wrist and elbow, and redirected him into the wall on her left. The impact was significant. He did not immediately rise.

The two roof positions discharged simultaneously. She was no longer where she had been. She had moved during the redirect, the building facade between her and the left roof position, the right position's angle compromised by the street geometry. The right position's technique struck stone where she had been. The left position couldn't find a line.

The three behind her reached her position. She met the first with a strike that her chassis delivered with the precision of something that had no uncertainty in its geometry. He folded. The second she caught and redirected into the third. Both went into the street.

The fourth and fifth (the remaining roof positions, now descending) she addressed before they landed, timing the intercept to their descent arcs. Two rapid controlled strikes. Both down but not dead.

The sixth and seventh had pulled back. She could see them recalculating at the far end of the street, the body language of people whose plan had been based on assumptions that were no longer valid.

She looked at them. They ran.

She stood in the empty street with seven cultivators in various states of incapacitation around her, the Voidheart Stone's carrying case in one hand and the Celestial Iron's crate in the other.

*Structural integrity: unchanged. Carrying cases: undamaged. No collateral casualties. Duration of engagement: eleven seconds.*

*The auction house's security perimeter extends approximately forty meters from the front entrance. They positioned their ambush at forty-one meters.*

She checked the nearest incapacitated combatant for identifying items and found, in addition to weapons and spirit stones, a sealed correspondence case.

She opened it. She read its contents in forty seconds.

Then she read them again.

---

The correspondence was between two parties, neither named directly. One referred to as *the Architect*, one as *Senior Brother Dao*. The content, decoded through the oblique language of people who expected their communications might be read by the wrong eyes, was unambiguous:

The formation arrays maintaining the earth dragon's sleep cycle had been deliberately triggered. The timing had been coordinated to coincide with a period of known weakness in the major sects' Nascent Soul populations.

*The Architect* had identified this window. *Senior Brother Dao* had executed the triggering.

The goal: *with the guardian sects depleted and demoralized, the consolidation can proceed on the accelerated timeline.*

There was a reference to *the next phase*, which was less specific but carried urgency. *"The Eternal Flame Order's losses have been significant. Now is the time."*

The sect insignia on the correspondence case: partially obscured, but partially was not fully, and her optical sensors could reconstruct the complete pattern from the visible fragment.

Iron Cauldron Sect. A mid-tier sect with a reputation for aggressive territorial expansion and a history of operating in legal grey areas.

She replaced the correspondence in its case and put it in her inner pocket.

She looked at the men on the ground around her. The older one was regaining consciousness (she had timed this deliberately). She looked down at him.

*"The Iron Cauldron Sect,"* she said. *"Tell them Baihua of the Eternal Flame Order sends her regards. And tell them that the correspondence they sent through this channel has been read."*

He stared at her.

*"Go,"* she said.

He went.

---

She made the return journey in two and a half days. Not running (running attracted attention). Something between her standard walking pace and the fastest pace she could sustain without it registering visually as inhuman.

She reviewed the correspondence three more times during the journey. *The Architect* remained anonymous. The correspondence predated the dragon's awakening by approximately two years, which meant the operation had been planned well in advance, by someone who knew the Nascent Soul population count of the major sects two years ago and could project its trajectory forward.

The mountain came into view on the second afternoon, the familiar profile of the Eternal Flame Order's peak.

She walked through the sect's lower gate in the early morning of the third day.

Chen Mao was sitting on the steps outside the outer sect's main hall with a bowl of something hot in his hands.

He looked at her.

*"You're back,"* he said.

*"Yes."*

*"You were supposed to be six weeks. It's been two and a half."*

*"Circumstances changed."*

He looked at her, at the carrying cases, at the pace she had arrived with. He set down his bowl and stood up.

*"How bad?"* he said.

*"I need to speak with Elder Shen immediately,"* she said. *"Where is he?"*

*"Fifth tier. He started moving around two days ago."* Chen Mao fell into step beside her. *"Baihua."*

*"Yes."*

*"How bad?"*

She looked at him. He looked back with the expression that meant he was asking not as a bystander but as someone who considered her concerns his concerns.

*"The dragon was deliberate,"* she said. *"And it's not finished."*

He was quiet for three steps.

*"Right,"* he said, and kept walking.

---

Elder Shen was in his garden on the fifth tier. He was sitting upright (an improvement from two weeks ago, though the deliberateness of his posture indicated the meridian damage was still present). He looked at her when she came through the garden's entrance. Then he looked at her more carefully, at the pace, at the two weeks early.

*"Sit down,"* he said.

She sat. She produced the correspondence case from her inner pocket and placed it on the table between them.

*"The Iron Cauldron Sect,"* she said. *"They ambushed me after the auction. Seven cultivators, including a Core Formation practitioner. I found this on one of them."*

He looked at the case. He picked it up. He read.

She watched him read, the small and precise changes in his expression as his three-hundred-year mind encountered information that was both confirming what he had suspected and worse than he had expected.

He set the case down.

*"The Architect,"* he said.

*"Unknown to me. The reference to 'consolidation' and 'the next phase,' given the timing and the assessment of this sect's current state..."*

*"They're coming,"* he said. *"Yes."* He looked at the garden. *"They've been waiting for this window for two years. They manufactured it."* A pause. *"And now they know you have this correspondence."*

*"I told the man I let go that I had read it. I gave him my name."*

*"You wanted them to know you have it."*

*"I wanted them to know the element of surprise regarding the correspondence is gone. It changes their calculation."*

*"It also marks you permanently as an enemy of the Iron Cauldron Sect."*

*"They tried to kill me first,"* she said. *"The sequence of enmity is clear."*

He looked at her for a long moment with something she had seen in Chen Mao's face many times but that wore differently on a face with three centuries behind it, the expression of someone who has watched a person step into the portion of the world where consequences compound.

*"I need to convene the elder council,"* he said. *"Today."* He stood, with the careful precision of someone managing a recovering body. *"You should attend."*

She looked at him. *"I am an outer sect disciple."*

*"You are the disciple who brought us the intelligence that tells us we are about to be attacked,"* he said. *"The council can object to your presence if they feel strongly about it."*

He picked up the correspondence case and walked toward the hall. She rose and followed him. In the garden, Chen Mao was waiting at the entrance, his bowl still in his hand.

She met his eyes as she passed.

*"Go cultivate,"* she said.

*"Absolutely not,"* he said, and followed them both.
